## 2. PORTER'S FIVE FORCES ANALYSIS (Turkish Aviation Sector)

### 2.1 Rivalry Among Existing Competitors (THYAO vs PGSUS)

**Score: 4.0 / 5.0** (HIGH, but moderated by segmentation)  
**Trend:** ↔ STABLE (unchanged past 12 months)

**Evidence:**
- **Duopoly Structure:** THYAO 49.1% + AJet 14.3% (group) = 63.4% combined market share; PEGASUS 20.3%. Remainder: foreign carriers, charters ~16%
  - *Source: [CAPA Centre for Aviation analysis, August 2024](https://centreforaviation.com/analysis/reports/turkish-airlines--pegasus-airlines-competitive-equilibrium-712900)*
- **Market Segmentation:** THYAO dominant in long-haul (international FSC network), PEGASUS in short-haul point-to-point (LCC). Limited direct route overlap.
- **Capacity Growth:** Both airlines expanding aggressively. THYAO ASK +8.7% (March 2026), PEGASUS ASK +14-16% guidance (2025). Capacity outpacing demand growth (~9% sector growth), potential yield pressure.
  - *Source: [THYAO March 2026 Traffic, AeroHaber](https://aerohaber.com/turkish-airlines-announces-march-2026-traffic-figures/)*
- **Load Factor Divergence:** THYAO 78-84% vs PEGASUS 87.7% (2025). PEGASUS efficiency advantage reduces direct price competition.
  - *Source: [Pegasus Q4 2024 Investor Presentation, March 2025](https://www.pegasusinvestorrelations.com/medium/image/2024-q4-investor-presentation_1496/view.aspx)*
- **Price Competition Limited:** Both maintain distinct segments (THYAO premium/business, PEGASUS economy); reduces head-to-head pricing wars.

**Assessment:** Rivalry is **moderate-high** within segments but **segmented by business model.** Competition is not all-out price war; more of managed competitive equilibrium.

### 2.2 Threat of New Entrants

**Score: 2.0 / 5.0** (LOW)  
**Trend:** ↓ DECLINING (slot constraints tightening)

**Evidence:**
- **Capital Requirements:** Commercial aircraft list prices $75-150M per unit. THYAO fleet 528 aircraft (CapEx intensive). PEGASUS has 120+ aircraft on order.
- **Regulatory Barriers:** 
  - Turkish SHGM operating license required (complex certification)
  - EU carriers need Turkish traffic rights (ICAO bilateral/EU agreements)
  - Istanbul IST airport slots: Nearly at capacity (slot rationing implemented Q1 2026); new entrants would compete with incumbents
    - *Source: [Istanbul IST capacity analysis, Air Service One, March 2026](https://airserviceone.com/new-route-of-the-day-29-march-2026-lufthansa-between-munich-and-istanbul-ist/)*
- **Existing Entrants:** EU carriers (Lufthansa, KLM, Air France) operate to IST via bilateral agreements but do not base aircraft there. Turkish domestic market closed to foreign operators.
- **Recent Activity:** No new Turkish-based airline launched 2024-2026. Consolidation risk limited (THYAO state-owned, PEGASUS family-held).

**Assessment:** **New entry risk is LOW.** Slot constraints at IST, regulatory complexity, and capital requirements create high barriers. THYAO's state ownership and PEGASUS's established position deter new entrants.

### 2.3 Threat of Substitute Products/Services

**Score: 2.5 / 5.0** (MODERATE-LOW)  
**Trend:** ↔ STABLE

**Assessment:** **Substitution threat MODERATE-LOW.** Rail expansion could impact domestic short-haul (İstanbul-Ankara, İstanbul-İzmir) in 5-10 years, but unlikely to materially harm either airline's core network (international hub-and-spoke depends on geography, not substitutable).

### 2.4 Bargaining Power of Suppliers

**Score: 3.5 / 5.0** (MODERATE-HIGH)  
**Trend:** ↑ INCREASING (fuel price volatility, labor pressure)

**Evidence:**
- **Aircraft Manufacturers (Oligopoly):**
  - Only Airbus and Boeing dominate commercial aircraft. THYAO on Airbus 90-aircraft order (A321neo). PEGASUS on A320 family orders.
  - Limited negotiating power; manufacturers can shift delivery schedules or pricing.
  - *Source: [THYAO capacity guidance, AeroHaber](https://aerohaber.com/turkish-airlines-earned-2-2-billion-profit-in-2025-90-new-aircraft-arriving-in-2026-2091/)*
- **Fuel Costs:**
  - Jet fuel = 25-30% of total operating costs (globally ~26% 2025, per IATA; Turkish carriers likely similar given USD/TRY pass-through).
  - *Source: [IATA 2026 Outlook](https://www.iata.org/en/pressroom/2025-releases/2025-12-09-01/)*
  - WTI/Brent price swings create volatility (hedging provides only partial offset).
  - Fuel surcharges available but customer resistance (especially LCC segment).
- **Labor Costs:**
  - THYAO: Unionized workforce (Hava-İş agreement 2026-2027: CPI+3% wage increases). Rising labor cost pressure.
    - *Source: [KAP Watch output, labor agreement notification]*
  - PEGASUS: Younger, smaller workforce; cost advantage but also unionization pressure.
  - Turkish labor market tightening (tourism sector competing for same workforce).
- **Airport Infrastructure:**
  - Turkish airports (DHMİ — state-owned monopoly). Landing, handling, slot fees controlled by monopolist.
  - Istanbul IST capacity constraints increase negotiating leverage of airport operator.

**Assessment:** **Supplier bargaining power MODERATE-HIGH.** Aircraft manufacturers have limited flexibility, fuel costs volatile, labor unionized with CPI+ clauses, airport monopoly control critical. Airlines cannot easily switch suppliers; cost pressures significant.

  - Higher loyalty (corporate contracts, frequent flier status).
  - THYAO Star Alliance membership increases switching costs (frequent flier mileage, premium seating, lounge access).
  - Pricing power better here; less price-sensitive.
- **Cargo Segment (THYAO Turkish Cargo, growing segment):**
  - More concentrated buyer base (logistics companies, e-commerce platforms). Better pricing power than passenger.
  - Seasonal volatility (higher in Q4 holiday, lower in Q2-Q3).

**Assessment:** **Customer bargaining power MODERATE-HIGH.** Leisure segment highly price-sensitive; online distribution lowers switching costs. Capacity growth puts yield pressure on both carriers. Business/cargo segments offer better pricing power but represent smaller revenue share.
